Why "nutrient gaps" matter more than calorie counts

Most tracking apps stop at a calorie total, which tells you almost nothing about whether a diet is actually working. Two people can eat the same number of calories and end up with completely different outcomes, because one of them is quietly low on iron, fibre, or vitamin D week after week.

Nutriguide was built around a simple idea: track the nutrients that actually affect how you feel, not just the number that affects the scale. When a gap shows up three weeks running, that's a pattern worth acting on — and worth a specific, edible suggestion rather than a lecture.

A single low day rarely matters. A pattern almost always does.

That's the difference we try to surface — quietly, in the background, every time you log a meal.
